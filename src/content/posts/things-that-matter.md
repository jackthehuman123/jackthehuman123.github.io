I've started to realized that software engineering in this generation now focuses on 3 main pillars:

## Leverage (own the stack)

Agentic coding is here to stay. As much as I was an anti of AI coding, I've come to the realization that software development has "evolved" into running agents, harnesses and evals well enough to do the work of a small team. 

It is an engineer's instinct to have the urge to control every line of output coming from "letters predictors" that has no sense of reasoning. "What if the syntax fail?", "What if this logic is implemented incorrectly?". The truth is, leveraging agentic workflows produces an amount of outcomes that cannot be fully managed by humans, **we need to accept that**. 

Accepting means creating autonomous measures such as evals, testings, etc.. practices that have been used in software engineering from time to time, with a good balance of human intervention. 

## Depth (own the knowledge)

One may ask "If AI acts as a wikipedia / source of truth, what's the point of knowing things in depth, when knowledge is one prompt away?" 

No one can ensures that within the next few years, LLMs won't be able to take care or writing eval loops and carry out testing for all parts of an application. In fact, we can just tell the agents to run a bunch of scripts, covering all edge cases and branch, log it out, write tests, reimplement code, and loop it, and we'll **perhaps** get the desired results.

So, "we" "tell the agents" will be my current answer to this section. It is true that one day, an agent will be capable enough to write all sort of testing they can come up with. But with domain knowledge and technical depths, we humans can verify whether all those test cases are enough, and carry out more iterations.

## Judgement (own the direction)

Let's be honest, everyone wants to make money. From a businessman's perspective, what's a more fascinating idea than having an autonomous machine that pumps out half-assed ideas while generating a good cash flow? 

Slop wins on volume. A clever person understands that the more you make, the odds tilt in your favor

Judgement wins in quality, retention and brand images. I believe that the edge that a well-built, well-defined product wins just by being... well-made. 

So really, it boils down to what you want as a builder. Either is wrong, if you care more about the short-term reward, slop it is. If you're more of a long-term player, make better judgements. Or, be hybrid.

Agents can build almost anything now. Deciding what to build, however, is the choice we need to make.
